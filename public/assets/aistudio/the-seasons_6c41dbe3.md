# The Seasons

<!-- chapter: 01-introduction | pages: 6-6 -->

Halice is the start of the cycle, defined
by ideals of Beauty, Freedom, Survival
and Progress. Embodied by idealists and
custodians, it seeks to preserve the good
parts of the past while violently
restructuring the bad ones. This noble
pursuit of iteration upon the familiar is,
however, easily hijacked by those who
would seek to profit from its efforts -
Survival can easily be twisted into greed,
Freedom can be taken to represent
brutal natural law.
Vamor follows once the new world order
has been established. It is represented by
the ideals of Ambition, Cunning, Passion
and Design. The pioneers and architects of Vamor aim to take advantage of this new
prosperous reality to undertake grand projects and bend the world to their will.
They believe that the wondrous and grand ends justify the often unfortunate
means. Those who would not perish for the grand project may as well be worthless.
Bruma is the inevitable realisation of what had occurred, a wholly human reaction
to the collateral damages of Vamor. It channels Altruism, Empathy, Diligence and
Safety through its folk heroes and martyrs in hopes of mending the world’s woes.
However noble its intent, Bruma’s self-destructive tendencies ultimately make it a
deeply flawed and unhealthy worldview. In the extremes, it promotes martyrdom to
the point of eating itself alive.
Trumst, armed with the knowledge of the cycle’s failed experiments, takes a final
rational approach to the issue of the human experience. Unity, Order, Curiosity and
Truth guide its rulers and peacekeepers in the creation of a fair and optimal world.
While efficient and orderly, this ideology wishes above all else to turn humans into
perfect cogs to fit its grand system, often failing to meet individual needs due to its
ruthless focus on reason. Eventually, the machine is torn down by those who would
not submit to its iron fist, and the cycle begins anew…
