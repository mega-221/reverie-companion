# Flow of The Game

<!-- chapter: 02-playing-the-game | pages: 11-11 -->

Most gameplay bits of Reverie can be described as scenes. The Narrator will
typically present the environment as well as point out a few details or points of
interest before handing off the spotlight to the players. From that point onwards,
the players will, as seems natural, interact with this scene through their characters.
They may investigate, talk to non-player characters, discuss things amongst one
another and so on.
Scenes should always have a goal of some kind, be it a piece of exposition or
character building, an opportunity to gain some kind of advantage or item, a
combat encounter or anything else that would make sense in the story. When the
goal of the scene is reached, the Narrator should transition the game to a new one.
Whenever a player character attempts to do something uncertain or even remotely
difficult, the Narrator should present the character with a skill check of an
appropriate challenge level. This will be explained in the next section.
Whenever the Narrator or a player encounters a situation where they aren’t certain
of what should happen or what to decide, they may make a destiny roll. To do so,
they determine two or more possible options and assign them to the numbers or
ranges of a d20 die and roll it. This random number generator is a simple way for
Narrators to introduce volatility to scenes and a useful way to resolve potential
decision uncertainties for players.
