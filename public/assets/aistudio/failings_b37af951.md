# Failings

<!-- chapter: 04-characters-in-depth | pages: 36-37 -->

Failings are the other side of the coin when it comes to convictions. They represent
negative personality traits that manifest when characters defy their convictions and
wreak havoc on their ability to make decisions.
They work almost exactly like convictions, presenting a new rule the character
must follow while punishing them with crisis should the rule be defied.
Conflicting Oaths
Should two or more convictions or failings ever come into conflict, the one with the
higher rank has higher priority and adhering to it will not result in any DP loss.
Choosing to adhere to a conviction or failing of lower rank will result in a DP loss
equal to the difference between the ranks of the stronger conviction or failing and
the one that was chosen.

Removing a Failing
There are multiple ways to go about lowering the rank of a failing:
● Pay an amount of DP equal to the rank of the associated conviction
● Lower the rank of the associated conviction by 1 if its rank is at least as high
as that of the Failing
● Spend long periods of downtime working on getting rid of a failing (4 weeks
per rank)
Which Failing do I get?
Determining which failing a character gets should be determined by the character’s
player and the Narrator. The failing should be in some way tied to the action that
caused it and the player should provide the Narrator with their input on how they
imagine their character reacting. The Narrator still has the final say on which failing
is manifested.
There are a total of 8 different failings:
● Greed - I will put my own needs and desires above those of others.
● Paranoia - I cannot entrust others with matters that affect me.
● Despair - I cannot further my own wellbeing.
● Masochism - I will put myself in harm’s way.
● Wrath - I will punish all who oppose me.
● Arrogance - I will do everything in my power to avoid humiliation.
● Apathy - I cannot consider the wellbeing and interests of others.
● Cowardice - I will do everything in my power to avoid harm.
