# Abilities In Detail

<!-- chapter: 03-survival-and-combat | pages: 28-28 -->

Now that the combat roll and basic combat terms have been covered, we can take a
better look at abilities and how they should be read. The symbol in the top left
describes the amount of ACP required to perform it and the color of the symbol
describes its type (attack/defensive/utility). Below that, there is the ability subtype
that further signifies the specifics of using this ability (for example a “1H Melee
Attack” is an attack ability that can only be performed with one-handed melee
weapons. Specific requirements are listed in the row after that one. The stats line
always covers the SN cost of using the ability while attacks go on to describe the
ACC and DMG modifiers of the attack while defensive abilities modify EVA. Finally,
the effect describes any specific effects of the ability that have not yet been
covered.
>> Reckless Attack —> Ability speed (ACP cost), type and name
Melee/Ranged Attack —> Ability subtypes
Requirements: none —> Weapon type/other requirements
| 0 SN | -1 ACC | +1 DMG | —> SN cost and ACC/DMG/EVA modifiers
Effect: —> Unique effects
-
Each character always has access to all of the basic abilities and abilities provided
to them by their class. In addition, characters get to have 6 active abilities from any
of the discipline abilities they know. Using inactive but known abilities costs a
destiny point. It is important to note that no ability may be performed more than
once per combat round unless stated otherwise in its effect.
Capstone Abilities
Characters may also cast abilities from their adopted disciplines that are a rank
above their current access. Doing so costs 1 destiny point. This is meant to allow for
the players to test out new abilities before learning them as well as provide
expensive power spikes in dire situations
