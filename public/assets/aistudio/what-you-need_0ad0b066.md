Narrator. A person playing as the world and all of its non-player inhabitants, the
main storyteller and the head judge of all rules. It is their duty to feed the story to
the players and to guide them on their journey. They will roleplay as various
characters and they will describe how the world reacts to the players and their
actions.
Players. The main cast of the story. Each player will take on a role of their own
character(s) and navigate the world and the story through their perspective. They
will make important decisions and will bend the world to their will through their
convictions. It is recommended that an average party consists of no more than 4
players, although more could be had with some difficulty. The aim of this system is
to allow for meaningful roleplay, so it comes naturally that this will be better
showcased when each character has more time to shine. My personal preferred
party size is 3 players due to the asymmetry this creates in party disagreements.
This Rulebook. It contains the vast majority of what is needed to understand the
game’s mechanics and all the information a player would need to play the game.
Dice. You will need three 6-sided dice and one 20-sided die for each player. Only
one set is really required to play but it is just convenient for everyone to have their
own dice. The Narrator may benefit from having a few extra d6.
Character Sheets. The sheets showcasing the characters’ stats, either in a paper or
digital format. You will often reference these during gameplay.
The Narrator should also look into these:
Battlemaps. While playing without them is possible, battlemaps are an integral part
of the tactical combat in Reverie and are heavily recommended. If playing in person,
they can be hand drawn or printed. If playing online, a virtual tabletop is
recommended. It is always nice to have pretty battlemaps, but functional sketches
will do the job just as well. If you want a free tabletop that works great for online
play and can also be used for combat when playing in person, I recommend you
check out Owlbear Rodeo. It is what I use for my games.

Sound System. Having a way to play ambiance, music and other sound effects will
greatly enhance your players’ immersion. Depending on the group, having recurring
character themes and tone songs could greatly enhance the entire experience.
Notes. A way to take notes of what happens throughout the story is heavily
recommended.
Narrative Module. Unless the Narrator is making a completely new narrative, it is
recommended that they look into prepared stories and change them to make them
their own. For newer Narrators, this is an excellent way to get a feel for Dunyor as
modules are designed to be non-linear in how they present their situations and can
easily be modified to suit any Narrator’s authorial voice. This practice is heavily
encouraged for those familiar with the system and is the biggest strength of the
TTRPG medium.

What you should read as a player
As a player, you need to be aware of Reverie’s basic mechanics, have a rough idea of
what different character progression options exist, and have a very limited
knowledge of the available items, mostly concerning your own character.
Those unfamiliar with the system’s basics should fully read chapters 1-3 as they
explain all of the basic mechanics to a satisfactory degree.
Those who are ready to make their own character should fully read chapter 4 as
well as skim over chapters 5-7. Chapter 5 contains a catalogue of classes, chapter 6
a catalogue of disciplines and abilities while chapter 7 contains the catalogue of all
aspects. These comprise the majority of what makes a character unique, and should
be read with the goal of finding interesting features for your character.
Finally, chapter 8 contains detailed lists of items and explains some additional item
mechanics like crafting and brewing. You don’t need to read through all of that, but
you will certainly find something useful in regards to any particular character in
there, ranging from different weapon options to entire systems like piloting
constructs.
What you should read as a Narrator
Everything, though it should also be noted that you don’t really need to have an
in-depth understanding of all classes, abilities or items or memorize them by heart.
Rather, you should seek to have a full mental image of the book and the system in
order to be able to better guide your players and answer any questions they might
have.
