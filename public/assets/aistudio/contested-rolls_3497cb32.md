# Contested Rolls

<!-- chapter: 02-playing-the-game | pages: 17-18 -->

When a skill check would act in direct conflict with another character, it is called a
contested roll. In contested rolls there usually isn’t a set CL.
Let’s take an attempt to sneak by a guard with the Stealth skill as an example:
The person attempting to sneak by using Stealth will have to make a roll against the
guard who is using their Perception skill. They will both roll their respective skill
checks and those results will be compared.
If the Stealth roll result is strictly higher than the Perception roll result, the
character successfully sneaks by. However, should the roll be a tie or should the
Stealth roll achieve a lesser result than that of the Perception roll, The guard will
spot the character.
In contested rolls there is an active side (the one initiating the situation) and a
passive side. The active side needs to score higher than the passive side to win.
Ties are counted in the passive side’s favor.
Some examples of common contested skill matchups may include:
● Perception vs Stealth
● Persuasion vs Willpower
● Deception vs Intuition
● Athletics vs Athletics
● …
Sometimes, the Narrator may decide that a contested roll is a waste of time and
may provide a static CL instead of rolling for the defender. In those situations it is

important to keep in mind that this is still a contested roll and that a tie results in a
victory for the passive side.
In some situations there might not be an apparent passive side. When the rolls
result in a tie in those situations, just repeat the roll until it is no longer a tie.
