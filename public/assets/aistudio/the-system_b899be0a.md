# The System

Reverie is a game about tortured people dealing with their internal struggles in a
world that manifests them into reality. These “souls” navigate unstable pockets of
space where sentient minds dictate reality, they sacrifice and fight for those they
represent and they meet horrible fates when they get stuck between pragmatism
and integrity. The setting of Dunyor is a mix of gothic and new weird fantasy with
various philosophical and metaphysical themes layered throughout. Reverie will be
at its best in long lasting campaigns centered around the moral and ethical
alignment of its main cast as they bend or break in tragic fashion.
Reverie is a game about many things, but the most important are the following:
● Conviction - Reverie was designed to make roleplaying a character with
defined beliefs as fun and engaging as possible. Your characters’ personal
beliefs will influence everything from skill checks to combat, from mundane
interactions to the fabric of reality.
● Sacrifice - Where other narratives may focus on noble, capable heroes,
Reverie instead showcases what heroism costs. Heroes do exist, but they
rarely if ever end up being rewarded for their pursuits. Their powers always
come at a horrible cost, and by choosing to be heroic they are going against
their own well-being and happiness in a desperate attempt to provide them
for others. More often than not, characters in Reverie are just competent
people put into situations way above their capabilities.
● Sorrow - While you will feel many things throughout the game, the emotion
that will consistently be the most central is sorrow. Sadness has a way of
lowering our guard and making us more open to deep thought and
reconsideration of our beliefs. We will explore it and we will consider its
painful realities. These stories will be hopeful tragedies.

It should also be noted what Reverie is explicitly not:
● A crunchfest - despite having many rules for many things, Reverie’s rules are
there to serve the narrative rather than the other way around. They are to be
ignored if they would conflict with the story or hinder the tone, though they
are designed with the intent to avoid this as much as possible. The various
systems and procedures are there to create friction and tension in order to
set the mood at the table. The most impactful moments of gameplay will be
narrative situations enabled and foretold by mechanical realities.
● A social relaxation game - More often than not, Reverie expects you to be
fully invested into the world and the story. Roleplay is heavily encouraged
because it naturally fits the melancholic tone. The game’s central themes are
heavy and demand to be taken seriously by the players. If you are sensitive to
stories about hardship and death or if you are looking for a simple way to
pass time with a group of friends, I recommend you take a look at any of the
great games designed to provide just that.
● An escapist fantasy - While beautiful and hopeful moments exist and are
essential to the experience, they are not the norm. If you want to play a game
to escape from your real life, you should be aware that many topics in Reverie
will pull you into just that. Politics and ideology play a big part in most
narratives and the game intends to ask you, the player, how far your own
beliefs could stretch and how horrible they could become before you stop to
reconsider. This may not suit everyone, and for those I suggest a more
light-hearted game.
***
With all that in mind, if you are not repulsed by these commitments, if you believe
that you can handle some introspection and if you desire to be a part of a game and
story that takes itself and its audience seriously, enter and be welcomed.

The World
The various planes of Dunyor are split apart by unreality, a void where the laws of
space and time stop functioning. The planes themselves are stable pockets of
existence shaped by their distance from the world’s Origin point and by the Seasons
its inhabitants adhere to.
The world’s Seasons define its conditions, each represented by 4 differing
convictions, each manifesting in a different way. Those devoted to them, Virtues,
have the ability to channel them directly and bend the fragile reality. Individuals
embodying each of them the most are known as Purities and they are the closest
thing to gods this world has seen. Their bodies are warped and their minds
inflexible, they act as the living executors of the will of the metaphysical Seasons.
421 years after the event that shattered Dunyor, the inhabitants of Eterion,
confident that they have enough knowledge on how to traverse the void, send out
the First March, an expedition into this strange new world, the first of many, in
order to survey its people, to secure new resources in order to avoid the collapse of
their plane and to find out what happened all those centuries ago.
Dunyor’s stories are most often told through the eyes of the souls, the members of
Eterion’s First March, (in)voluntary expeditioners from various planes of Dunyor
who were brought to Eterion by one of Bruma’s Purities at the moment of their
death. Their minds have been torn and they can hardly remember their past lives.
Their second chance came at a great cost, and they will be the ones to document
and shape this cruel place in hopes of repaying their debt.
