# The Combat Roll

<!-- chapter: 03-survival-and-combat | pages: 26-27 -->

Whenever any kind of attack ability is performed, a combat roll is made to
determine its success, not unlike a skill check that is usually used to perform
non-combat tasks. Combat rolls do not use skills or attributes, but some weapons
have attribute requirements in order to be used effectively.
Each combat roll will include 3d6, your character’s Class rank and any additional
modifiers the ability you are performing may provide.
Combat Roll
[ 3d6 + Class Rank + MOD ]
The attribute relevant to the weapon is different for each unique weapon and listed
in its stats. Combat rolls are not rolled against a set CL but rather the target’s
evasion.
Accuracy
Accuracy (ACC) is the most common modifier that can affect a combat roll. It is
gained or lost depending on the effects of each ability. Each point of accuracy
represents +1 that is added to the combat roll.
Damage
Damage (DMG) represents harm. Most attacks tend to deal some, and taking
damage is reflected in losing HP should it not be stopped by armour or some other
means.

Evasion
Evasion (EVA) is a measure of a character’s ability to defend themselves in combat,
be it by dodging or parrying attacks. Its purpose is to avoid DMG and it acts as the
first layer of defense against all attacks - it is the CL that all attacks targeting the
character roll against. Its value starts at 10 for regular characters and can be further
increased through gear and abilities.
Armour
Whenever a character would take DMG, if they are wearing armour or have
otherwise obtained armour points (AP) in some way, the DMG is reduced by their
AP. Armour points act as a form of permanent damage reduction but they can be
depleted through the use of abilities. Depleted AP are restored at the end of a
combat encounter.
Armour is split into light, medium and heavy categories. The categories, while
generally defining the armour’s effectiveness, also have unique requirements to be
worn and associated downsides:
● Light Armour: no requirements, no penalties
● Medium Armour: Carry 3, disadvantage on Stealth checks
● Heavy Armour: Carry 6, disadvantage on Stealth checks and -1 tile movement
Characters who are wearing a piece of armour without meeting the Carry skill
requirement receive a -2 penalty to combat rolls and a -1 tile movement penalty.
Damage Overflow
When a character successfully performs an attack, depending on their roll they may
inflict additional DMG depending on its result. For each 2 points the roll achieved
over the target EVA, +1 DMG is added to the attack. This represents the fact that
more accurate strikes are obviously going to hurt more. For example, an attack that
rolls 12 against a target with 10 EVA will deal +1 DMG, while an attack that rolls 16
against a target with 11 EVA will deal +2 DMG. These bonuses are added to the
weapon’s base damage value to achieve a final DMG of the attack.
